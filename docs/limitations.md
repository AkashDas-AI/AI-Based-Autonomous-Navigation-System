# System Scope and Known Limitations

## 1. Scope of the Baseline Milestone

The initial milestone establishes a verified, lightweight 2D simulation baseline intended for rapid algorithmic verification, path planning, and trajectory control without heavyweight robotics or physics engines.

---

## 2. Technical Assumptions & Current Limitations

### 2.1 Environmental Assumptions
- **Planar 2D Surface**: All calculations assume a flat, 2-dimensional plane ($Z = 0$). Elevation, slope angles, curb heights, and 3D terrain topography are not modeled in the 2D grid representation.
- **Moving Obstacle Modeling**: While Milestone 4 introduces moving dynamic obstacles (crossing, corridor, chokepoint) into the real-time simulation and DWA local planner, they operate in 2D Euclidean space as circles with known radius and deterministic motion patterns. Arbitrary non-convex shapes, 3D articulated humans, and deformable objects are not modeled.

### 2.2 Discretization Artifacts
- **Grid Resolution Bounds**: Global A* search is constrained by the grid discretization ($\delta = 0.5\text{ m}$). Paths exhibit piecewise diagonal/orthogonal segments rather than continuous Bézier splines, requiring the controller or subsequent post-smoothing steps to produce curvature-continuous trajectories.

### 2.3 Kinematics vs. Vehicle Dynamics
- **Unicycle Kinematics**: Motion is simulated via a kinematic unicycle model ($\dot{x} = v \cos \theta, \dot{y} = v \sin \theta$). Lateral tire slip angles ($\alpha_f, \alpha_r$), suspension dynamics, weight transfer, and surface friction coefficients ($\mu$) are not modeled.
- **Velocity Regimes**: The kinematic model remains physically accurate for low-to-moderate speed regimes ($v \le 5\text{ m/s}$), but dynamic bicycle models with brush tire equations will be necessary for higher-speed highway navigation.

### 2.4 State Estimation & Sensor Noise Modeling Boundaries
- **Estimation Accuracy vs. Trajectory-Tracking Distinction**: State estimation accuracy ($\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k - \mathbf{p}_{\text{true}, k}$) measures how closely the internal feedback pose reflects physical vehicle reality. In contrast, trajectory-tracking performance ($\mathbf{p}_{\text{true}, k} - \text{segment}(W_i, W_{i+1})$) measures how closely the vehicle physically follows the planned A* path. In `ground_truth` feedback mode, estimation error is $0.0\text{ m}$ by construction, but tracking error (orthogonal CTE) remains non-zero due to geometric corner smoothing and lookahead dynamics. Under noisy EKF feedback, state estimation errors propagate directly into the control loop, causing cross-track tracking error to increase (e.g. from $0.071\text{ m}$ to $0.158\text{ m}$ in `simple_obstacles`).
- **Synthetic Sensor Noise vs. Physical Sensor Realities**: The simulated sensor suite introduces realistic Gaussian white noise, systematic wheel scale factor errors, random-walk gyro bias drift, and decimated 1 Hz GNSS fixes with signal outage intervals. However, **these are idealized mathematical simulation models and must not be equated to physical hardware sensor deployments.** Physical MEMS IMUs suffer from non-linear temperature drift, vibration rectification error (VRE), cross-axis sensitivity, and acoustic noise. Physical GNSS receivers experience urban multipath reflections, atmospheric delays, dynamic satellite constellation geometry (HDOP/PDOP dilution of precision), and non-line-of-sight (NLOS) signals.
- **Planar Kinematic Process Model**: The EKF state vector $\mathbf{x} = [x, y, \theta, v, b_g]^T$ assumes planar 2D kinematic unicycle motion. Wheel slip, lateral skidding, tire scrubbing during sharp turns, and 3D terrain pitch/roll attitudes are not estimated by the filter.
- **Observability During Signal Outages**: When GNSS position fixes are unavailable (outage mode), absolute position and heading drift become unobservable from wheel speed and single-axis gyro alone. The system relies strictly on dead-reckoning, causing error covariance $P$ to grow monotonically over time until a valid GNSS fix is acquired.
- **First-Order Taylor Series Linearization**: As an Extended Kalman Filter, the transition model relies on analytical first-order Jacobians ($F = \partial f / \partial \mathbf{x}$). For extreme initial angular uncertainty or high angular rates during GNSS-denied maneuvers, non-linearities can degrade filter optimality, where Unscented Kalman Filters (UKF) or factor graph smoothers would offer stronger convergence guarantees.

### 2.5 Computer Vision & Perception Pipeline Assumptions
- **Synthetic Test Accuracy vs. Real-World Camera Performance**: The reported sub-5cm centroid accuracy and $90\%$ IoU are measured exclusively on deterministic, controlled synthetic fixtures with ideal step-function contrast, known geometry, and zero optical distortion. **These metrics must not be interpreted as real-world centimeter-level camera accuracy.** In real-world operational deployments, cameras suffer from radial/tangential optical distortion, rolling shutter artifacts, motion blur, exposure latencies, non-uniform solar illumination, specular reflections, dynamic shadows, and camera pitch perturbations caused by vehicle acceleration and suspension dynamics.
- **Classical vs. Semantic Processing**: The detection module uses classical OpenCV image processing (Otsu thresholding, morphological filtering, spatial moments). It does not perform semantic object classification or deep learning-based detection (e.g. YOLO, Mask R-CNN). All segmented foreground regions are treated uniformly as generic untraversable obstacles regardless of whether they represent cardboard boxes, pedestrians, or solid barriers.
- **Planar Ground Assumption (Homography / IPM)**: Inverse Perspective Mapping via homography assumes all projected pixels lie on a strictly planar ground surface ($Z = 0$). Three-dimensional obstacles extending above the ground plane will undergo perspective parallax distortion and appear elongated along the camera radial line of sight. Furthermore, non-flat terrain (slopes, ramps, curbs, potholes) violates the $Z = 0$ ground plane invariant, producing severe spatial mapping distortions.
- **Static Sensor Calibration**: Camera intrinsic parameters, mounting heights, pitch angles, and homography projection matrices are assumed constant and pre-calibrated without real-time online camera extrinsic recalibration.

### 2.6 2D Kinematic Simulation vs. Physical Robot Reality
- **Simulation Environment Characterization**: The interactive Pygame visualizer and underlying simulation engine constitute a **lightweight 2D kinematic simulation environment** developed for algorithmic development, planning verification, and control logic exploration. **It must not be evaluated as a validated physical robotic platform.**
- **Absence of Tire Dynamics and Friction**: Motion is integrated strictly using unicycle kinematics with zero wheel slip, zero lateral skidding, infinite friction coefficient ($\mu = \infty$), and instant steer rate achievement without dynamic tire brush deformation or lateral force saturation ($F_y \le \mu F_z$).
- **No Actuator Dynamics or Latencies**: Commanded acceleration and yaw rate are achieved instantaneously up to configured kinematic limits ($a_{\max}, \omega_{\max}$) without motor back-EMF, gearbox backlash, communication bus latency (e.g. CAN bus jitters), or throttle/brake delay.
- **Idealized Collisions**: Obstacle collisions are evaluated geometrically as analytical intersections (circle-to-AABB). There is no rigid body restitution, impulse transfer, structural deformation, or contact friction.
- **Absence of Environmental Disturbances**: The environment lacks external forces such as wind gusts, ground slope gravity components, floor unevenness, carpet drag, or wheel alignment biases.

### 2.7 Dynamic Obstacle & Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) Assumptions & Limitations
- **Deterministic Motion vs. Stochastic Intention**: Current dynamic obstacle models use deterministic kinematics (`linear_ping_pong`, `circular`, `waypoints`, `constant_velocity`). In physical deployments, human pedestrians and vehicles exhibit stochastic, multi-modal, and non-deterministic behavior requiring probabilistic state tracking (e.g., Extended Kalman Filters or Particle Filters) and intention-aware trajectory prediction.
- **Local Minima and Concave Obstacle Trapping**: DWA is a short-horizon ($2.0\text{ s}$) local trajectory optimizer. In deep concave geometric traps (e.g., U-shaped corridors or dead-ends containing moving obstacles), DWA may experience local minima or limit cycles without global path replanning.
- **Lookahead Sub-Goal Sensitivity**: DWA extracts its local heading target from the global A* route. If an oncoming obstacle directly occludes the sub-goal, heading alignment scores degrade and can induce conservative slowdowns or stops.
- **Discrete Velocity Space Sampling**: Velocity space is sampled over a discrete grid ($11 \times 15$ candidate trajectories). Highly constrained gaps between multiple close obstacles may be missed if the discretization is too coarse.
- **Absence of Social Conventions**: DWA evaluates physical geometric clearance and forward velocity. It does not model human social conventions (e.g. passing on the right, yielding rules), which can lead to symmetrical head-on hesitation ("freezing robot problem") without protocol-based or game-theoretic negotiation.

### 2.8 Temporal Continuous Collision Checking Assumptions & Physical Boundaries
- **Linear Relative Segment Approximation**: The continuous relative swept-distance check models the relative motion between robot and obstacle over each discrete $\Delta t = 0.1\text{ s}$ time step as a straight-line segment in difference coordinates. While exact for constant linear velocities, high angular rates within a single $100\text{ ms}$ interval create slight trajectory curvature. For $\Delta t \le 0.1\text{ s}$ and $|\omega| \le 1.0\text{ rad/s}$, the maximum mid-chord sagitta error is $< 2.5\text{ mm}$, well within the configured safety margin ($0.1\text{ m}$).
- **Circular Disc Footprint Invariant**: Both robot and dynamic obstacles are modeled geometrically as 2D circular discs. Real vehicles possess rectangular footprints with non-uniform overhangs, and pedestrians exhibit articulated limbs, gait swaying, and dynamic body rotation.
- **Simulation Collision Checking vs. Physical Safety**: The simulation collision checker operates with exact, noise-free ground truth positions and deterministic obstacle trajectories. In real-world physical systems, sensor estimation noise, LiDAR/camera tracking latency, perception false negatives, and unexpected pedestrian course deviations require stochastic safety buffers and formal control barrier functions (CBFs).

### 2.9 High-Fidelity Simulation Bridge Scope & Physical Reality Boundaries
- **Mock Test Adapter vs. Physical/External Simulator Connection**: The `OfflineTestAdapter` in `autonomous_nav.bridge` is an **in-process mock adapter developed strictly for automated testing, CI, schema enforcement, and failure injection**. It does NOT connect to an external running CARLA, Gazebo, or ROS 2 process, nor does it run a multi-body rigid physics engine. It provides the software integration boundary and data contracts, but real external 3D simulator connection requires dedicated deployment on appropriate OS/GPU hardware.
- **Planar 2D LiDAR Slicing vs. 3D Volumetric Point Clouds**: The implemented `LaserScanObservation` and `laser_scan_to_grid_map()` model a single-plane horizontal radial scan. Physical 3D LiDARs (Velodyne, Ouster) generate dense 3D point clouds subject to ground reflections, negative obstacles (ditches, curbs), atmospheric backscatter (rain/fog), and non-uniform ray densities. Directly projecting unsegmented 3D returns into a 2D occupancy grid causes false-positive ground obstacles without ground-plane filtering (e.g. RANSAC).
- **Planar Kinematics vs. 3D Vehicle Attitude Dynamics**: The bridge contract accommodates 3D state $(x, y, z, \text{roll}, \text{pitch}, \text{yaw}, v_x, v_y, v_z)$, but the internal navigation planners (`AStarPlanner`, `PurePursuitController`, `DWAPlanner`) and unicycle model project this state strictly onto the $Z=0$ horizontal plane. Suspension roll, pitch during braking, bank angles on curved roads, and uneven terrain topography are not incorporated into the 2D path tracking or obstacle avoidance algorithms.
- **Absence of Real-World Physical Robot Validation**: Establishing standard message contracts and verifying an offline adapter does not validate physical vehicle hardware. Real-world validation requires testing on actual mobile base actuators (CAN bus motor controllers, steer-by-wire latency), physical sensor calibration (hand-eye camera calibration, IMU-LiDAR extrinsic matrices), and handling unpredictable ambient lighting, GPS multipath in urban canyons, and variable tire-ground friction.

### 2.10 Real-World Dataset Integration Boundaries & Constraints
- **Authentic TUM RGB-D Archive & Frame Extraction**: The official sequence archive `rgbd_dataset_freiburg2_pioneer_slam2.tgz` ($1,183,281,970\text{ bytes}$, $\approx 1.10\text{ GB}$) was acquired and verified via multipart download (`scripts/download_tum_archive.py`), and 5 authentic 16-bit depth frames were selectively extracted into `data/tum_rgbd/depth/` with cryptographic manifest tracking (`data/tum_rgbd/manifest.json`).
- **Unverified Extrinsic Calibration Hypothesis**: TUM published camera intrinsics but no physical rigid-body extrinsic transformation linking the 100 Hz MoCap marker tree to the Kinect optical center. Baseline mounting parameters ($d_x=+0.15\text{ m}, d_y=0.0\text{ m}, d_z=+0.40\text{ m}$, zero pitch/yaw/roll) remain a project engineering hypothesis, whose empirical sensitivity bounds were formally quantified in Milestone 7.5.
- **Laboratory MoCap Frame vs Geographic ENU**: The TUM ground truth trajectory is defined in an indoor laboratory coordinate system established during 10-camera infrared system calibration. It is **not** geographically aligned to East-North-Up (REP-103 ENU).
- **Temporal Association vs Hardware Clock Synchronization**: Nearest MoCap sample residuals ($0.016\text{--}1.568\text{ ms}$) reflect the 100 Hz MoCap sampling granularity ($\Delta t \approx 10\text{ ms}$); they do **not** prove physical hardware clock synchronization between the Kinect sensor and the MoCap tracking computer.
- **2D Obstacle-Grid Projection vs Full 3D Volumetric Mapping**: The current pipeline projects 3D depth returns into 2D discrete `GridMap` occupancy via height slicing ($Z_b \in [0.05, 1.20]\text{ m}$). It performs obstacle endpoint rasterization (point hits) and does not perform ray-marching free-space clearing, probabilistic log-odds mapping, or full 3D volumetric OctoMap representations.
- **Frame-by-Frame Mapping vs Full Visual SLAM**: Processing 5 authentic depth frames demonstrates calibrated depth projection and mapping feasibility; it does **not** constitute a complete real-time visual-inertial SLAM deployment (e.g. ORB-SLAM or RTAB-Map). Volumetric 3D mapping and full visual odometry remain designated as **optional post-release enhancements**.
